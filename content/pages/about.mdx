Kiterae Vertical exists for the part of the vertical gardening conversation that usually gets skipped after the renderings look convincing.

The market already has tower systems, container farms, home devices, greenhouse operators, and sleek installation photos. What commercial buyers need is a way to evaluate the operating model behind the greenery: service coverage, crop planning, uptime, reporting, harvesting, maintenance, and accountability.

## The editorial position

We treat vertical gardening as a managed asset. The plants matter, but so do the routines that keep them healthy, the data that proves performance, the field team that responds when something changes, and the buyer-side workflow that turns a growing system into a leasing, dining, wellness, or sustainability advantage.

That is why this publication focuses on vertical farming as a service. Many commercial teams want the outcomes of indoor agriculture: fresher ingredients, visible sustainability, biophilic design, tenant differentiation, and supply resilience. Few want to hire, train, and manage a farming operation.

## Who it is for

The first reader is the commercial real estate asset manager or developer trying to make space more valuable without adding another fragile amenity. They need a living feature that can be marketed, maintained, and measured.

The second reader is the hospitality operator who needs reliable access to premium, perishable ingredients and a credible zero-mile story for guests. They care about freshness, consistency, and operational simplicity more than farming hardware.
