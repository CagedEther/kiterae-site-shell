Searches for the best vertical gardening systems often lead to equipment comparisons: tower versus wall, hydroponic versus aeroponic, container versus indoor rack, automated versus manual. Those comparisons matter. They are not the whole decision.

For commercial buyers, the more important comparison is product versus service.

## Product-led models transfer work

A product-led model sells the equipment and often the software subscription. The customer then owns much of the operating burden: crop selection, seeding, harvesting, cleaning, monitoring, maintenance, troubleshooting, staff training, and vendor coordination.

That can work when the buyer wants to build internal farming capability. It is a poor fit when the buyer's actual goal is a tenant amenity, a menu supply program, a visible sustainability feature, or a low-friction brand experience.

## Service-led models sell the outcome

A service-led model should include hardware, software, crop operations, maintenance, and reporting in one operating structure. The buyer is not trying to become a grower. The buyer is trying to get dependable value from a growing system.

This does not mean the hardware is irrelevant. It means hardware should be judged by how well it supports the service promise: access, cleaning, monitoring, crop health, replacement speed, aesthetics, energy use, and field service.

## Compare by unanswered work

When reviewing options, ask what is still left for your team:

- Who decides what to grow?
- Who handles seeding and harvest?
- Who monitors nutrient levels, lighting, irrigation, and crop health?
- Who responds to failures?
- Who maintains the visual standard?
- Who supplies reports for leadership, tenants, guests, or investors?

The best answer may be a system. It may be a service. It may be a hybrid. The point is to compare the full workload, not only the equipment sheet.

## Next action

Create a two-column evaluation: "included by provider" and "owned by us." Put every operational task in one column. If the internal column is long and your team does not want to farm, the product is probably not the right model.
